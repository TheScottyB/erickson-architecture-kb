# Des Plaines Mall

Three-level urban shopping mall in downtown **Des Plaines, IL**, the centerpiece of the city's [[Des Plaines Superblock|Superblock]] urban-renewal initiative. Designed by **[[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]]** for developer **J.R. Gottlieb & Co.** Opened October 6, 1977; closed October 31, 1996; demolished beginning June 1998.

## Timeline

- **1972** — Planning starts as part of broader [[Des Plaines Superblock]] vision
- **1973** — Des Plaines Mall Corporation formed
- **June 1976** — Construction begins
- **October 6, 1977** — Mall opens
- **1979** — Lower-level "Olde Center Street" added (turn-of-the-century theme arcade)
- **1983** — Mall management company goes bankrupt
- **Spring 1992** — Anchor tenant Spiegler's Department Store closes
- **1995** — City of Des Plaines takes control with intent to condemn
- **October 31, 1996** — Mall closes
- **June 1998** — Demolition begins

## Behrel Parking Plaza

A 300-car, 4-story cement parking deck on **Ellinwood St**, built as part of the same scheme, was widely **blamed for the failure of the mall** — its scale and pedestrian-hostile design discouraged downtown foot traffic.

## Significance

The Des Plaines Mall is the textbook cautionary tale of 1970s suburban urban-renewal: a downtown department-store-anchored mall, planned to compete with the new regional malls (Woodfield opened 1971), that struggled almost from the start, took down its management company in less than a decade, and was condemned by the same city that championed it. Of the [[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]] Superblock work, only [[701 South Lee Street]] survives.
