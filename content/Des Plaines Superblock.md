# Des Plaines Superblock

The **1970s urban-renewal megaproject** that re-platted an entire block of downtown **Des Plaines, IL** into a coordinated cluster of new commercial buildings and a parking deck, all designed (architecturally) by **[[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]]** for developer **J.R. Gottlieb & Co.** in partnership with the City of Des Plaines.

## Components

| Component | Note | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [[Des Plaines Mall]] | 3-level shopping mall, anchor of the scheme | **Demolished 1998** |
| [[701 South Lee Street]] | 10-story office tower with ground-floor retail | Standing (sole survivor) |
| Behrel Parking Plaza | 300-car, 4-story cement deck on Ellinwood St | Status unclear; widely blamed for the mall's failure |
| [[Lee Street Office Plaza (950 S Lee)]] | Originally "Le Ronde"; pre-dates the Superblock proper but part of the same E&S downtown footprint | Standing |

## Source Document

The **Des Plaines Public Library** preserves a captioned photograph spread from the **November 10, 1977 edition of the *Des Plaines Journal*** documenting the Superblock at the moment of completion:

> "Captioned photographs from the November 10, 1977 edition of the Des Plaines Journal showing the evolution of the Superblock development scheme in downtown Des Plaines. The photograph at the bottom right shows representatives of the Des Plaines Mall management company, the architects and developer of the Superblock development, as well as Robert Spiegler of Spiegler's Department Store and Arthur Weiss of the First National Bank of Des Plaines. The mall and Superblock development failed to live up to expectations; the mall management company went bankrupt in 1983. Spiegler's Department Store went out of business in the spring of 1992."  
> — *Des Plaines Memory*

## Outcome

The Superblock failed commercially within a single business cycle:

- 1983 — Mall management company bankrupt
- 1992 — Anchor tenant (Spiegler's) closes
- 1996 — Mall closed by city
- 1998 — Mall demolished

What remains in 2026 is **701 South Lee Street** — making it the most important physical artifact of the [[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]] commercial body of work in Des Plaines.
