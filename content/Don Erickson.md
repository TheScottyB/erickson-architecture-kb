# Don Erickson

**b.** August 29, 1929, Chicago, IL · **d.** October 24, 2006, Barrington, IL (age 77, multiple myeloma)
**Full name:** Edward Donald Erickson (Swedish family; called by middle name per tradition)
**Source:** *Biography of Edward Donald Erickson* by Karyn L. Erickson (daughter), 2007 — see `assets/don-erickson-bio.pdf`

Architect, Chicagoland. Partner in **[[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]]** with [[Dennis Stevens]], formal partnership **1965**–mid-1992. Erickson's design vocabulary leaned on Frank Lloyd Wright's organic-modernist principles — soaring volumes, natural light, post-and-beam structure — applied across **residential** (see [[Don-Erickson-Architecture]]) and **commercial** work.

## Career Timeline

- **1948–1951** — Taliesin apprenticeship (both Taliesin West, Scottsdale and Taliesin East, Spring Green). Both he and cousin Richard Erickson accepted on same day: Dec 15, 1948.
- **1951** — Private practice begins; first project: Inverness, IL home for parents (still standing)
- **1965** — Formal partnership with [[Dennis Stevens]] as **[[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]]**
- **mid-1992** — Partnership ends; Don practices solo from Barrington home studio
- **1993** — Diagnosed with multiple myeloma; one of 1% to survive 10+ years after diagnosis
- **1998** — Co-founded I.C.A.R.A. (later the **Association of Licensed Architects**, 6,000+ members)
- **2006** — Died at Barrington home; buried White Cemetery, Cuba Road, Barrington

## Frank Lloyd Wright Connection — RESOLVED

**Both** Erickson and Stevens were Taliesin apprentices. Don apprenticed **1948–1951**; Stevens joined the same program. Stevens was specifically cited as Wright's "youngest-ever apprentice" (at 17). Don played piano for the Wrights at Taliesin and was a personal friend. The earlier conflation is resolved — both stories are true.

## Most Notable Works

- **Indian Lakes Resort** — 250 W. Schick Rd, Bloomingdale, IL 60108 *(his best-known public work)*
- **Bird Cage Apartments** — 6900 N. Ridge Blvd, Chicago (Rogers Park)
- **Singer Bowl, 1965 World's Fair** — Flushing, Queens, NY *(now Louis Armstrong Stadium, US Open)*
- **The Round House** — 1958 AIA Homes for Better Living Award; featured in Chicago American, Playboy

## Des Plaines Work

As partner in [[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]], Don is credited on:

- [[Lee Street Office Plaza (950 S Lee)]] — originally "Le Ronde"
- [[701 South Lee Street]] — the lone surviving building of the Superblock
- [[Des Plaines Mall]] — opened 1977, demolished 1998

## Style Hallmarks

Carried over from the residential catalog (see [[Don-Erickson-Architecture]]):

- Chalet Modern / MCM / Mid-Mod-Med
- East–West gable skylights, diamond skylights
- Floor-to-ceiling stone fireplaces
- Post-and-beam construction, pecky cedar wood ceilings
- Wraparound decks, sunken living areas
- Brick floors
