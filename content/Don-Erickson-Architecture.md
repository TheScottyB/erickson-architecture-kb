# Don Erickson — Architect

**Full name:** Edward Donald Erickson
**Born:** August 29, 1929, Chicago, IL (Swedish immigrant family)
**Died:** October 24, 2006, Barrington, IL (age 77) — multiple myeloma
**Buried:** White Cemetery, Cuba Road, Barrington, IL
**Primary source:** *Biography of Edward Donald Erickson* by Karyn L. Erickson (daughter), June 16, 2007 — `assets/erickson-don-biography-karyn-erickson-2007.pdf`

## Firm

**Erickson and Stevens, Inc.**
**Partner:** Dennis Stevens (formal partnership: **1965** – mid-1992)
**Offices:**
- 6645 Simpson, Morton Grove, IL (~1954, earliest)
- Busse Highway, Des Plaines (shared with father Gunnar)
- 640 Pearson Street, Des Plaines
- 950 Lee Street (Le Ronde building), Des Plaines
- 393 S. Milwaukee Ave, Wheeling (next to Bob Chin Crab House, early 1980s)
- Barrington home studio (mid-1992 onward, solo)

## Biographical

**Parents:** Eric Gunnar Erickson (Sweden, 1922) and Ebba Linnea Erickson nee Larson (Olmstad, Sweden, 1923). Swedish immigrants settled in Chicago.
**Cousin:** Richard Erickson — chief draftsperson 1953 through mid-1992.

Don grew up in Elmwood Park, IL (father Gunnar ran a custom spiral staircase business). Enchanted by a FLW-designed home near his school. After one year at University of Illinois Navy Pier architecture program, his professor said *"If you like [Wright] so much, go to him!"* Don did.

**Taliesin apprenticeship: December 15, 1948 – 1951** — at Taliesin West, Scottsdale, AZ (accepted with cousin Richard as a condition). Met wife Shirley Mae Dahlke (fine artist, pianist) at Taliesin.

After Taliesin: worked for Schewier & Eating, Frank Louis Glick, Ragnar Benson, and E. Zari & Son (including supervising construction of a FLW-designed home for the Glores in Lake Forest, IL). Married Shirley Dec 13, 1952. Private practice established **1951** — first project: Inverness, IL home built for his parents.

Formal partnership with **Dennis Stevens** (another Taliesin apprentice) in **1965**. Don also became a developer and construction manager, not just architect. Home in **Barrington 1966–2006** was his "laboratory" — he built and rebuilt it to test designs.

**Tornado:** April 21, 1967 — leveled the Barrington home. Don suffered 10-day amnesia from shock. Rebuilt with signature **Jamaican roofs**, moved into new home 1968.

**Partnership ended mid-1992.** Don fell off his roof same year, broke his back. Diagnosed with **multiple myeloma 1993** — one of 1% of patients to survive 10+ years. Continued working from his Barrington studio.

**1998:** Co-founded I.C.A.R.A. (later the **Association of Licensed Architects**), now 6,000+ members. Also served as AIA arbitrator.

**Dennis Stevens** — Wright's **youngest-ever apprentice** (at 17, in 1948). NATO base architect in Italy. Continued practicing in Boise, Idaho with son Sean after 1992.

The firm operated as **design + build**, developing a standing team of contractors who understood their non-traditional methods.

## Signature Design Elements

- **"Jamaican roof"** — their most distinctive invention; an inverted gambrel roof with a bubble skylight at the apex. Made with massive curved glulam beams (custom-fabricated in Missoula, MT) in a compression ring–expansion ring system. Interiors open into soaring volumes of pecky cedar. Neighbors often called these **"pagoda houses."**
- Chalet Modern / MCM / Mid-Mod-Med vocabulary
- East–West gable skylights · Diamond skylights
- Floor-to-ceiling custom stone fireplaces
- Wraparound decks · Sunken living areas
- Post-and-beam construction · Brick floors
- Grand soaring volumes · Natural light emphasis

## Notable Works

**Most notable (per biography):**
- **Indian Lakes Resort** — 250 W. Schick Rd, Bloomingdale, IL 60108 *(considered his signature public work)*
- **Bird Cage Apartments** — 6900 N. Ridge Blvd, Chicago IL (Rogers Park)
- **Singer Bowl, 1965 World's Fair** — Flushing, Queens, NY (client: Singer Sewing Machine Co.) — *now Louis Armstrong Stadium, US Open*
- **The Round House** — 1958 AIA Homes for Better Living Award winner; featured in Chicago American, Chicago Daily News, Playboy

**Residential:**
- Inverness, IL — first home (1951, built for his parents; still occupied)
- Barrington home — his personal "laboratory" (built 1966, tornado 1967, rebuilt with Jamaican roofs 1968)
- Jerry Seidel House, Park Ridge, IL (1968)
- 1962 home on Park Lake with private beach, Park Ridge
- Emanuel Feingold House, Highland Park, IL (1958) — *landmarked*
- Multiple "Jamaican roof" homes: Palatine (incl. Dennis's own home), Winnetka, Wooddale, Riverwoods, Highland Park
- Restaurant, River Road, Mt. Prospect (now in disrepair)
- Projects in Jamaica

**Commercial:**
- Decoral Corporation (now U.S. Music Corporation) — 444 E. Courtland St, Mundelein, IL
- Information Resources, Inc. — 150 N. Clinton St, Chicago (interior + entranceway)

**Civic / Commercial (Des Plaines "Superblock" downtown redevelopment):**
- **Des Plaines Mall** — 700 Pearson St, 185,000 sq ft, opened Oct 6 1977, *demolished 1998*
- **701 S. Lee St** — 10-story Superblock Complex (1976–78); *only surviving Superblock building*
- **950 S. Lee St (Le Ronde → Lee Street Office Plaza)** — 1974; white brick, interior rotunda, bronze glass
- **Behrel Parking Plaza** — 300-car deck on Ellinwood St; *demolished*
- **Transportation Center** (1970s, unbuilt) — train depot, pedestrian overpasses, office buildings
