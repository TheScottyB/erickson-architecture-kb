# Jamaican Roof

The signature structural and aesthetic invention of [[Erickson & Stevens, Inc.]] — an inverted gambrel roof topped with a bubble skylight, producing soaring interior volumes flooded with natural light.

> "The Jamaican roof is something Don and I came up with together, and the idea came from some buildings we'd seen while working in Jamaica. The idea is actually pretty similar to a gambrel roof, but upside down and backwards."
> — [[Dennis Stevens]], *Modern Illinois* interview

## Construction

- **Beams:** Massive curved glulam beams custom-fabricated in Missoula, Montana
- **System:** Compression ring–expansion ring; beams curve down from a central ring and rest on perimeter posts
- **Decking:** Double tongue-and-groove on top of the beams — ceiling and roof are the same element
- **Skylight:** Bubble skylight at the apex of the ring
- **Interior:** Pecky cedar wood ceiling; spaces open into the full soaring volume beneath
- **Finish:** Wood shingles on exterior

An early discovery: once the decking weight was distributed on the curved beams, they lifted off their intermediate support columns. The columns were eliminated from later designs, leaving the interior entirely open.

## Nickname

Neighbors and even some owners called these **"pagoda houses"** — the upswept roofline resembles traditional Japanese architecture at first glance.

## Known Examples

- **Riverwoods, IL** — one of the first built with a Jamaican roof
- **Palatine, IL** — multiple homes, including Dennis Stevens' own home
- **Winnetka, IL**
- **Wooddale (Wood Dale), IL**
- **Highland Park, IL**
- **Jamaica** — built in Jamaica itself
- **Mt. Prospect, IL** — restaurant on River Road (now in disrepair)
- **[[Don Erickson]]'s Barrington home** — rebuilt with Jamaican roofs after 1967 tornado; moved in 1968
- **[[Don-Erickson-Architecture|507 Park Plaine Ave, Park Ridge]]** — curved roof variant
