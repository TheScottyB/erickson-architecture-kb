# Singer Bowl

**Flushing Meadows–Corona Park, Queens, New York**
**Client:** Singer Sewing Machine Company
**Event:** 1965 World's Fair, New York

[[Don Erickson]] designed one half of the Singer Bowl for the 1965 World's Fair. The biography notes he spent nearly a year flying back and forth to New York City, in a plane chartered by Singer, during the design process (circa 1964, while his third child Elizabeth was born in September 1964).

> "So many modifications were made to the building that it barely resembles the structure designed for Singer."
> — *Biography of Edward Donald Erickson*, Karyn L. Erickson, 2007

## Current Status

The Singer Bowl was later renamed **Louis Armstrong Stadium** and became part of the **USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center** in Flushing Meadows. It served as a major venue for the **US Open tennis championships**. A new, larger Louis Armstrong Stadium opened in 2018; the fate of the original structure has evolved through subsequent renovations.

## Significance

This is Erickson's only confirmed project at an international event and his only major work in New York. The commissioning by Singer Sewing Machine Company places it in the same era as the firm's formation (Stevens partnership formalized 1965).
